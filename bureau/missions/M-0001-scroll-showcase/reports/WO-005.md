# Report — WO-005 — Spike: Scene 4 canvas cost at 1920 (D2)

STATUS: BLOCKED (the spike is complete and every acceptance criterion is met; the WO's own decision rule returns "none qualifies", which the plan says to file as a BLOCKED request to the CEO)
Engineer discipline: performance · Mission: M-0001 · Round: build

## Decision (what WO-010 builds on)
**Under the written rule, no D2 level qualifies at 1920.** The rule needs rAF p95 ≤ 20 ms and flushed draw p95 ≤ 6 ms. The best 1920 level, L2 (pitch +4, c3), measured rAF p95 50 ms and flushed draw p95 39.8 ms. Per plan.md WO-005, this is a **BLOCKED request to the CEO** (see Requests).

**Engineering recommendation, pending the CEO's ruling: build WO-010 at L0, the spec configuration (c1).** That means DPR cap 1.5 at ≥ 1440 (2 below), lattice 2 × dot pitch (72 at 1920), the 256-entry LUT, and redraw only on a p or ambient change. Why:
1. **No level passes, and the levels barely help.** L1 is the same canvas as L0 at 1920. L2 cuts flushed p50 by only 13% (30.4 → 26.5 ms) and coarsens the lattice everywhere. L3 helps only while admiring (rAF p95 50 → 33.5) and does nothing while scrolling.
2. **The 6 ms threshold can't be reached on this rasteriser by any geometry lever.** Anti-aliased path raster on SwiftShader costs about 5.4–6.4 ms per backing Mpx (d4, d2). Even the 375×812 mobile canvas (1.22 Mpx) has a flushed p95 of 16 ms (c5). Meeting 6 ms at 1920 would need a backing under about 1 Mpx, which is below DPR 1 (2.07 Mpx). A full-canvas rect fill costs only 1.5 ms (d1), so the cost is path rasterisation in software, not fill rate.
3. Shipping below the spec configuration wouldn't make the rule pass, and the slip policy forbids treating thresholds as valves. So the remaining question is about the threshold, not the art. That is the CEO's call.

## What I built
- `spike.cjs`: a self-contained CommonJS harness. It draws a representative Scene 4 canvas inline, with no `site/` imports: a seeded jittered lattice, per-facet fill plus a 0.5 px same-colour stroke, the OKLCH LUT, the band-quant lerp, 1 px white edges, the seven-band overlay and the white point. The formulas come from experience.md Scene 4 and §3.5.
- Each configuration gets 3 fresh-page runs of 120 measured rAF frames (plus 10 warm-up frames), with p and θ changing every frame.
- **Pass A** is the production-like loop with no readback. It records rAF deltas and the unflushed draw time.
- **Pass B** (R8) times the draw calls followed by `getImageData(0,0,1,1)`, which forces deferred raster into the timed span.
- Measured frames are a stress superset (every layer drawn every frame), so they are conservative. d2 confirms that spec-faithful hold frames cost the same (99% of c1).
- It covers the 6 mandated configurations, an extra c7 (D2 L3, 30 Hz admire), and 7 diagnostics: raster floor, spec-faithful hold, willReadFrequently, DPR 1 hold, no stroke, and colour-batched ×2.
- The script writes `results.json`, `results.md` (tables, the decision rule applied mechanically, and caveats) and two screenshots per configuration.

## Findings for WO-010 (relative costs, from the diagnostics)
- **The LUT matters for JS, not raster.** Unflushed p50 is 1.7 ms with the LUT against 2.0 with per-facet OKLCH (c1 vs c6). Keep the LUT; it is cheap insurance on slow CPUs.
- JS and command recording is small: unflushed p95 ≤ 3.1 ms at 1920. That fits the architecture's "S4 apply ≤ 6 ms" budget if measured unflushed, and misses it by about 8× if flushed on SwiftShader.
- **The per-facet stroke costs about 14%** (d5 vs d2). Keep it: it is the seam fix (polish item 8).
- **Do not batch facets by colour.** It is 12–18% *slower* when flushed (d6, d7), even though JS gets faster.
- `willReadFrequently` makes no difference (d3), so the readback is not distorting the backend comparison.
- Backing pixels are the lever that works: DPR 1 roughly cuts cost by 2.5× (c2 vs c1). But DPR 1 is below spec and is not a D2 level.

## Known gaps, risks, follow-ups
- All numbers are headless SwiftShader numbers. They show relative cost and are not a device claim. Real-GPU cost is unmeasured and likely far lower, but that is an inference.
- **Spec colour issue I noticed (not mine to fix).** The LUT hue is `255° + 150°·(n·L)` with |n·L| ≤ 0.6, so it spans 165°–345° only. The p 0.5 "gold" facets and the p 0.75 "gold and vermilion" right half (hue ≈ 30–90°) can't be produced by the LUT as specified. The rendered hold frame is all blues, violets, teals and pinks (`c1-hold.png`).
