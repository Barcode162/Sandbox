Generated by `spike.cjs` on 2026-11-20T14:21:02.971Z. Playwright 1.63.0, headless Chromium 157.0.8103.78. 120 measured rAF frames per pass, 10 warm-up frames discarded, 3 runs per configuration on fresh pages; percentiles are over the frames of all runs pooled (nearest rank). Times in ms.

## What was drawn
A self-contained Scene 4 canvas in its own page (no `site/` code): ink ground; a mulberry32-seeded (`0xB3E7`) jittered lattice (±14 px · J), each square split on its "\" diagonal into two triangles, each drawn as its own filled path plus a 0.5 px same-colour stroke; colour from a 256-entry OKLCH → sRGB LUT indexed by n·L (c6: converted per facet per frame instead), lerped toward the band token of its centroid by `quant`; one path of 1 px white lattice edges; the seven-band overlay; and the white point as a radial gradient. Parameters follow the experience spec formulas, but the measured frames are a **stress superset**: every layer draws every frame (facet alpha ≥ 0.85, edge alpha ≥ 0.2, bands ≥ 0.25, quant ≥ 0.15), so no measured frame is cheaper than a real worst frame. In scroll cadence p sweeps 0.25 → 0.92 over the run and θ also carries a breathing offset, so p and θ change every frame.

Two passes per run, each on a fresh page, because a readback every frame changes what the browser does with the canvas:
- **Pass A (rAF)** is the production-like loop: draw in the rAF callback, no readback. It gives the rAF-delta numbers and the *unflushed* draw time (JS plus command recording; raster is deferred).
- **Pass B (flushed, R8)** times `performance.now()` around the draw calls **followed by `ctx.getImageData(0,0,1,1)`**, which forces the deferred rasterisation to finish inside the timed span.

## What these numbers measure and do not
They come from Playwright's headless Chromium shell in a 4-CPU container with no GPU. Canvas 2D is rasterised by **SwiftShader**, a software rasteriser, and there is no real compositor or display: rAF deltas are the headless shell's frame scheduling, not a monitor's refresh, and a delta of about 16.7 ms means "kept up with the 60 Hz begin-frame clock", nothing more. The flushed draw time includes the synchronous readback of one pixel, so it slightly over-counts raster cost; the unflushed time under-counts it, because raster happens later off the timed span. A readback every frame can make Chromium move the canvas off its accelerated path, which is why rAF deltas come from a separate pass without readback. Real GPUs rasterise filled triangles far faster than SwiftShader, and laptop CPUs differ from this container, so use these numbers **only as relative costs between configurations** and as a conservative floor. They are **not** a frame-rate claim for any real device, and nothing here supports a "60 fps" statement.
