# Playbook — General (writing, research, plans, analysis)

For reports, research, documentation, plans, analyses, guides, stories, presentations, and anything whose product isn't primarily code.

## The experience is the reader's experience
The experience spec covers:
- **The reader:** who they are, what they already know, and what they should know, feel or do afterwards.
- **Structure:** the argument or narrative arc, and what the first paragraph must deliver.
- **Voice:** tone, with example sentences.
- **Form:** Markdown, a rendered HTML page, slides or a PDF, and its visual design if rendered.

## How the Bureau writes
1. **Scouts** research in parallel, one question set each, using primary sources.
2. **The architect** writes the outline and the argument structure. Each section is a work order with an owned file.
3. **Engineers** (disciplines: `research`, `technical-writing`) write sections in parallel.
4. An **editing** pass (`engineer / editing`) unifies them into one voice and removes repetition and filler.
5. **The red-team** fact-checks every claim, number and link against primary sources.
6. **The polish-critic** judges structure, clarity, flow and, if rendered, typography and layout.
7. **The inspector-general** audits citations: each one exists and says what the text claims it says.

## Standards
- Every non-obvious factual claim is sourced, preferably from a primary source. Numbers carry dates.
- Lead with the answer. The structure can be scanned.
- No filler, no hedging stacks, no generic padding.
- One voice throughout.
- Fiction and creative work are original in characters, worlds and text.

## Verification specifics
A link checker, a citation-audit table (claim → source → verified?), a render check if formatted, and a spelling and grammar pass.

## Reviewer checklist (writing addendum)
- Could the reader act on this tomorrow? What would they still have to look up?
- Is any sentence there only to sound thorough? Cut it.
- Do the headline claims survive the red-team?
